# Sports partner-finding apps and skill-rating systems (pickleball and racket sports)

Research date: 2026-11-24. Sources are mostly vendor help pages and App Store listings, so they show what vendors say their products do, not independent evaluation. Where a page carries a date it is given. Pages without a date were read live in November 2026.

## 1. Which apps match players today, and on what signals (skill, location, availability, court, club)?

### Takeaway
The market splits into three groups. Scheduler or session apps (PlayTime Scheduler, Pickleheads) match people to sessions at courts, with skill as a filter. Club or booking platforms (Playtomic, CourtReserve-type tools) match people into open matches at club slots, limited by a level range. Swipe or "dating-style" partner apps (Hitch, PickleMatchApp, Courtmate, PairUp, PaddleLink and others) match person to person on distance, skill and availability. Few apps use the court as the main way to find players. Favourite courts mostly show up as a filter (PickleMatchApp), not as the screen you start from.

### Cited Findings
- **PlayTime Scheduler** says it "is not a court reservation system – it's a way of inviting others to play". Its calendar shows play sessions created by other users. Players see where friends are playing or "choose sessions that best match your skill level". Rating options are self-rating and DUPR. Tiers are Free ($0/yr), Premium ($25/yr) and Power ($35/yr). It claims about 519,100 players (self-reported, date unclear). — [PlayTime Scheduler learn more](https://playtimescheduler.com/learn-more.php); [User Guide](https://playtimescheduler.com/user-guide.php); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/playtime-scheduler/id6504500852)
- **Pickleheads** has a court directory of "over 16,000 locations in the US, Canada, and worldwide", game finding "tailored to your skill level", and a scheduler that auto-sends invites, collects sign-ups and runs chat. It also offers business tools to reserve court space and set a format "in one click". Paid tiers are Plus, Pro and Ultra. — [Pickleheads App Store (CA)](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/id6448714446); [Pickleheads scheduler](https://www.pickleheads.com/scheduler); [Pickleheads build-a-business](https://www.pickleheads.com/build-a-business)
- Pickleheads already lists Victoria, BC courts, including Topaz Park, Juan de Fuca Recreation Centre, Victoria Pickleball Centre and The Courts at Uptown. — [Pickleheads Topaz Park](https://www.pickleheads.com/courts/ca/british-columbia/victoria/topaz-park); [Pickleheads Juan de Fuca](https://www.pickleheads.com/courts/ca/british-columbia/victoria/juan-de-fuca-recreation-centre)

- **PickleMatchApp** is pitched as dating-style. Its filters are skill range, age range, location radius and **favorite courts**. — [PickleMatchApp App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/picklematchapp/id6749190103)
- **Courtmate** offers location-based discovery and skill-level matching across tennis, padel and pickleball. **PairUp** shows nearby players and open matches on a map with a configurable search radius. **PaddleLink** finds local players by skill level and location. **Racquet App** posts and joins matches with "skill-aware matching". — [Courtmate](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6755203060); [PairUp](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6755626973); [PaddleLink](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6759000159); [Racquet App](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6772728823)

- Tennis apps include **TennisPartner** (discover nearby players, match requests, chat), **RacketPal** (find partners across sports), **PlayYourCourt** (partners "at your local courts") and **Match Point** ("challenge local players to improve your rating and climb your local ladder"). — [Tennis Partner](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tennis-partner/id784546333); [RacketPal](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1453817491); [PlayYourCourt](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1525178013); [Match Point](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6765685415)

### Inferences
- Nearly every partner app uses the same filter set: distance, skill band, availability, singles or doubles, and sometimes gender or age. These are the baseline any app is expected to have, so they do not set an app apart.
- Bink's court-first screen (tap a court, see its regulars and its "king of the hill") is closer to how Pickleheads and PlayTime work, where courts and sessions come first, than to the swipe apps. Among the listings reviewed, no app makes "who plays at this court" the main screen. The closest are PickleMatchApp's favourite-courts filter and PlayYourCourt's "local courts" framing.
- Playtomic's "request a place if outside level range" pattern suits Bink well. The skill gap acts as a soft limit that the player can override, not a hard block.
- Pickleheads already covers Victoria courts, so it is the likely competitor for court discovery locally. Bink's edge would be the people layer (regulars, king of the hill, ranked directory), not the court list.

### Gaps
- CourtReserve, Rec, TeamReach, Playo, PicklePlay and the DUPR app's own "find players" or club features were not checked directly in this session. No primary source was read for how any of them rank players.
- No app published its ranking or "for you" algorithm. All the matching logic above comes from marketing filter lists.
- Download or user counts for the newer swipe apps (Hitch, PickleMatchApp and others) were not found.

## 2. Rating systems: DUPR, UTR, Playtomic levels, Elo, Glicko-2, TrueSkill — what fits doubles and a small, sparse local base, and how is self-rating inflation handled?

### Takeaway
All of the main racket-sport ratings are, at heart, "actual versus expected result" systems, either Elo-like or weighted averages. Each pairs the rating with a separate confidence or reliability figure that grows with match count, recency and opponent quality. Results verified by an organiser count more than self-posted ones. For a small doubles pool, the published systems point to three choices. Keep a per-player rating plus an uncertainty (Glicko or TrueSkill style). Set team strength as the average or sum of the two players. Give low-confidence and self-posted results less influence.

### Cited Findings
**DUPR (pickleball)**
- Scale 2.000–8.000, with bands Novice, Intermediate, Advanced and Professional. New players start as **NR (Not Rated)**. A starting rating can come from a certified DUPR coach, an AI video assessment through partners such as PB Vision, or by claiming existing matches. — [DUPR How It Works](https://dupr.com/how-it-works)
- "Every match has an expected outcome based on the ratings of everyone on the court." Doing better than expected raises the rating and doing worse lowers it. **Team rating = average of the two players' DUPRs**, and **points scored** against expectation drive the change, not just win or loss. Each player's adjustment depends on their own match count and recency. — [DUPR How It Works](https://dupr.com/how-it-works)
- Singles, gender doubles and mixed doubles are rated separately. There are age-group ratings for 50–65+. — [DUPR How It Works](https://dupr.com/how-it-works)
- "Club and Tournament Matches carry more weight than recreational ones." Self-posted matches still count, which keeps the system open to non-tournament players. (Post dated May 9, 2024.) — [DUPR Integration, Integrity, and Info FAQ](https://www.dupr.com/vi/post/upa-integration-and-dupr-algorithm-faqs---all-you-need-to-know)
- Older DUPR material described it as a **modified Elo algorithm using the last 30 (singles) or 60 (doubles) eligible matches**. Current official pages no longer give these numbers. — [DUPR blog "Cracking the DUPR Code"](https://www.dupr.com/post/cracking-the-dupr-code-how-pickleballs-rating-system-shapes-the-game) via search summary; not stated on [DUPR How It Works](https://dupr.com/how-it-works)
- **Reliability Score** is shown as 1–100% next to the rating. "A higher Reliability Score means your DUPR is more likely to reflect your current skill level". A low score means the rating rests on "outdated or insufficient match data". — [DUPR How It Works](https://dupr.com/how-it-works)
- The December 19, 2024 update added "match count", "match recency" and "win intensity" (margin) to the algorithm and improved "initial ratings". It launched the Reliability Score and lets clubs **require a minimum reliability score** for events. It also imported historical results from Pickleballtournaments.com and Pickleball Brackets. — [DUPR 2024 Product & Algorithm Updates](https://www.dupr.com/post/2024-dupr-product-algorithm-updates)
- Sources conflict on whether reliability changes how far a rating moves. One DUPR article says players with higher reliability see smaller swings. A 2024 analytics post said reliability was "not yet integrated" into the rating. — [DUPR "Cracking the DUPR Code"](https://www.dupr.com/post/cracking-the-dupr-code-how-pickleballs-rating-system-shapes-the-game); [DUPR Reliability Score deep dive (search result; page now 404)](https://dupr.com/post/reliability-score--a-deep-dive-with-head-of-dupr-analytics-scott-mendelssohn)
- DUPR says it "actively works to identify and flag sandbagging to event organizers". It says sandbagging is more common where organisers use self-ratings instead of DUPR, and it recommends level-based events that use verified ratings. Manipulating ratings breaks its Community Guidelines and can lead to restriction or suspension. No detection method is published. — [DUPR FAQ (May 2024)](https://www.dupr.com/vi/post/upa-integration-and-dupr-algorithm-faqs---all-you-need-to-know)
- DUPR says over 15,000 event organisers submit results directly. — [DUPR How It Works](https://dupr.com/how-it-works)

**UTR / UTR-P**
- A UTR Rating is the **weighted average of up to 30 most recent match ratings within the last 12 months**. A match gets more weight when the opponent has a reliable rating (plays often) and when it is more recent. — [UTR Algorithm FAQ](https://support.universaltennis.com/en/support/solutions/articles/9000233354); [How UTR Works](https://www.utrsports.net/pages/how-utr-works)
- Players with fewer than about 5 results have a **"projected" (P) rating**, which becomes fully reliable after about 5 matches. — [UTR projected rating FAQ](https://support.universaltennis.com/en/support/solutions/articles/9000151963)
- **Verified UTR** counts only results from verified third-party events (sanctioned leagues and tournaments, school matches). Standard UTR also includes self-posted, casual and practice matches. — [UTR verified vs unverified (P) FAQ](https://support.universaltennis.com/en/support/solutions/articles/9000238457-what-s-the-difference-between-utr-p-and-verified-utr-p-); [USA Pickleball UTR-P FAQ](https://usapickleball.org/ufaqs/what-matches-count-towards-the-utr-p-rating-and-what-is-verified-vs-unverified/)
- UTR-P (pickleball) sources describe a window of "at least a year of results or the past 60 games". This conflicts with the 30-match tennis rule, and the current UTR-P rule was not confirmed. — [How UTR-P works](https://www.utrsports.net/pages/how-utr-p-works) (search summary)
- USA Pickleball has an FAQ titled "How does the UTR-P Rating solve for sandbagging?" The page returned 404 when fetched in November 2026, so its content could not be confirmed. — [USA Pickleball FAQ (404)](https://usapickleball.org/ufaqs/how-does-the-utr-p-rating-solve-for-sandbagging)

**Playtomic levels (padel, tennis, pickleball)**
- New players answer a **questionnaire that places them between level 0 and 7**. **Only competitive matches change the level**, while casual or friendly matches do not. A **reliability** figure grows with matches, and lower reliability means bigger level swings. A coach-run **leveling session** at a certified club sets reliability to medium (50%). — [Playtomic level system (help center; 403 on direct fetch, content via search summary)](https://playerhelp.playtomic.com/hc/en-gb/articles/43310980754193-How-the-Playtomic-level-system-works); [Playtomic Levels: ups & downs](https://playerhelp.playtomic.com/hc/en-gb/articles/19831827459345-The-Playtomic-Levels-ups-downs)
- Clubs can only run leveling sessions after at least one coach passes a certification exam. Playtomic says a level set too high or too low "leads to poor experiences for the player, their partner, and their opponents". (A page asset is dated about Nov 2025.) — [Playtomic new leveling process](https://product.playtomic.com/en-us/new_leveling_process)

**TrueSkill (Microsoft Research)**
- Skill is a Gaussian belief with mean μ and uncertainty σ. Default μ = 25 and σ ≈ 8.333, so a new player's displayed rating μ − 3σ = 0. — [Microsoft Research TrueSkill](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/trueskill-ranking-system/)
- **Team skill = sum of member skills.** The update uses only the team's result, and the change is split among teammates according to their σ. "Players who always team up together can't be individually separated." — [Microsoft Research TrueSkill](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/trueskill-ranking-system/)
- Games needed to identify skill: 2-player head-to-head about 12 games; 4 teams of 2 about 10; 2 teams of 4 about 46. Actual numbers "can be up to three times higher". — [Microsoft Research TrueSkill](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/trueskill-ranking-system/)
- Matchmaking quality is defined as the **draw probability** (0–1), and high uncertainty lowers match quality even when μ is equal. Leaderboards use the conservative μ − kσ (k ≈ 3). — [Microsoft Research TrueSkill](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/trueskill-ranking-system/)
- A comparison of rating systems notes that TrueSkill infers skills jointly for team games, while Glicko-2 rates individuals with no built-in team model. TrueSkill 2 adds computational complexity and is less transparent than classic systems. — [The Evaluation of Rating Systems (arXiv 2105.14069)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.14069)
- In team games, most rating algorithms sum member performance into team strength. A 2021 study argues this mis-models "carry" effects. — [arXiv 2106.11397](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.11397.pdf)

**Glicko-2 (Glickman)**
- Primary source: Glickman's "Example of the Glicko-2 system". — [glicko.net PDF](http://www.glicko.net/glicko/glicko2.pdf). **Caveat:** the PDF could not be machine-read this session. The following well-known parameters come from prior knowledge of that document and were not re-checked here. Rating r (default 1500), rating deviation RD (default 350), volatility σ (default 0.06), and system constant τ (suggested 0.3–1.2). Results are batched into "rating periods", ideally with about 10–15 games per player per period. RD grows during inactivity, so a rating "ages" back toward uncertain. r ± 2·RD is roughly a 95% interval.

**Self-rating inflation (community evidence)**
- Rated open-play labels drift as groups fill up. "4.0+" comes to mean "strong 3.5s and up", and "4.5" becomes "anyone confident enough to sign up". Self-ratings blend skills into "a flattering average". — [Pickleball Union: How true is rated open play](https://pickleballunion.com/how-true-is-rated-open-play-in-pickleball/); [Pickleball Union: Why your self-rating and DUPR don't match](https://pickleballunion.com/why-your-self-rating-and-your-dupr-dont-match/)
- A Reddit user is quoted calling 4.0+ open play "mostly a frustrating experience for everybody". One or two players out of their depth can derail an advanced session. Some facilities label courts by level ("3.5+ court", "beginners only"). — [The Dink: The harsh truth about 4.0 open play](https://www.thedinkpickleball.com/the-harsh-truth-about-4-0-open-play/)
- Pickleball Canada and USA Pickleball still publish self-assessment skill descriptions (2.0–5.0) for placing beginners. — [Pickleball Canada skill rating](https://pickleballcanada.org/?p=5067); [pickleball.com skill levels guide](https://pickleball.com/docs/en/article/pickleball-skill-levels-ratings-guide-from-20-to-50-explained.html)

### Inferences
- **Doubles in a sparse pool:** DUPR (average of the two players) and TrueSkill (sum, with σ-weighted split) both rate individuals inside a team. That is the right approach for rotating-partner recreational doubles. It breaks down only for fixed pairs, which TrueSkill says cannot be told apart.
- **Small or sparse base:** Systems with an uncertainty term (Glicko-2 RD, TrueSkill σ, DUPR and Playtomic reliability, UTR's "projected" flag) fit Bink better than plain Elo. They let a new player's first few results move them quickly without disturbing established players. Glicko-2's rating period batching assumes about 10–15 games per period, which a casual player in a small city may not reach. Short periods, or TrueSkill-style per-match updates, likely fit better.
- **Self-rating handling pattern:** In every system the self-rating or questionnaire is only the *prior*. It is shown with a "projected", "NR" or low-reliability flag, and confirmed results replace it. Bink could store `self_rating` as the prior μ with a high uncertainty. It could display "self-rated" or "DUPR-linked" badges, and switch to a computed Bink rating after about 5 confirmed matches (borrowing UTR's threshold).
- **Verification weighting:** All systems weight verified or organiser results above self-posted ones. In Bink, a match confirmed by both players is the most trusted result Bink can get (no third party). Its weight could rise further when opponents are well established (UTR's "reliable opponent" weighting).
- **Score margin:** DUPR and UTR both use points or games won, not just win or loss. Bink already records scores, so margin-based expected-vs-actual updates are possible.
- **Simple implementation route:** For a small Postgres backend, a Glicko-1/2 or simplified TrueSkill update in a SQL function or Edge Function triggered on match confirmation is feasible. Rating history goes in a separate table. The Supabase specifics are not researched here.

### Gaps
- DUPR's exact current formula, the match-window size, how reliability changes rating movement, and how sandbagging is detected are all unpublished. The DUPR Reliability deep-dive page returned 404.
- The Playtomic level help pages returned 403. Details come from search-result summaries of the help center and were not read in full.
- No head-to-head study was found comparing Elo, Glicko-2 and TrueSkill specifically for recreational pickleball doubles or small local pools.
- Glicko-2 default values are from prior knowledge, not a verified fetch this session (see caveat above).

## 3. Win streaks, "king of the hill", ladders and challenge systems: scoring, verification, disputes, sandbagging

### Takeaway
In recreational pickleball, "King/Queen of the Court" is an in-session promotion and relegation format: winners move up a court, losers move down. App-based **challenge ladders** have players challenge up a limited number of spots, require acceptance within a deadline (or a default loss), and have the winner report the score with opponent confirmation or a short auto-confirm window. Formal dispute processes are rarely published. Sandbagging is handled mainly by using verified ratings for level-gated events, not by detection.

### Cited Findings
- **King of the Court** (also called Up River/Down River, Waterfall or Ladder): after each game the winning team moves up a court and the losing team moves down. Partners often split each game. Some versions split winners across the net to balance play. Games are short, often to 11 or rally-scored, or timed with "most points when the session ends". End courts only move one way. — [playpickleball.com types of rec play](https://www.playpickleball.com/types-of-pickleball-rec-play/); [Pickleball Brackets "Up Down" manual](https://www.manula.com/manuals/pickleball-brackets/pbmanual/1/en/topic/up-down); [Skedge King of the Court](https://skedge-web.web.app/resources/king-of-the-court); [r2sports King of the Court](https://www.r2sports.com/site/r2kingCourt.asp)
- A mathematical simulation compared King-of-the-Court movement rules ("Full": winners up and losers down; versus winners staying, or top-court losers sent to the bottom) to see how they sort players by skill. — [DeFord, King of the Court analysis (PDF)](https://drdeford.github.io/files/KC.pdf)
- Pickleheads markets organiser formats called "Claim the Throne" and "Gauntlet" (king-of-the-hill style). — [Pickleheads Claim the Throne](https://www.pickleheads.com/organize/watch/claim-the-throne); [Pickleheads Gauntlet](https://www.pickleheads.com/organize/watch/gauntlet)
- A Pickleball Canada club (South Cowichan, on Vancouver Island near Victoria) publishes event descriptions that include these formats. — [South Cowichan Pickleball Club event descriptions](https://secure.pickleballcanada.org/club/south-cowichan-pickleball-club/33024/1-event-descriptions/7175)
- **Challenge ladder rules (example):** challenges are limited to **3 spots above** the challenger's position. An unaccepted challenge becomes a **default loss for the challengee at 11-0, 11-0, 11-0**. All players must report scores and view standings in the Global Pickleball Network app. — [Luna Sol challenge ladder rules](https://sites.google.com/view/lunasolsportspbladder/challenge-ladder-rules)
- **Spanish Fork (UT) challenger rules:** the challenged player must accept or deny in the app, and the winning team must report the score within a set window (about 2 hours per search summary). — [Spanish Fork Pickleball Challenger Rules (PDF)](https://spanishfork.org/docs/ParkRec/Sports/Pickleball/Pickleball_Challenger_Rules.pdf)
- **Score verification pattern:** one ladder league (Dinkzter-based) uses verification by "1 player per side" with a **3-hour auto-confirm**. — [Trackie-hosted league guide PDF (2026-05)](https://files.trackie.com/uploads/redactor/2026-05-18-16-30-14-322499808-1081.pdf)
- A tennis ladder app publishes rules for reporting and challenges. — [Tennis ladder rules](https://romrell4.github.io/tennis-ladder-ws/rules.html)
- UTR Sports Pickleball Flex Leagues publish rules for self-scheduled league matches. — [UTR Pickleball Flex League rules](https://support.universaltennis.com/support/solutions/articles/9000226226-pickleball-flex-league-rules-regulations)
- Pickleball.com describes how facility desks handle on-court disputes ("getting players to the desk"). — [pickleball.com: Two disputes, two outcomes](https://pickleball.com/blogs/two-disputes-two-outcomes-getting-players-to-the-desk-when-things-go-sideways)
- **Sandbagging** is described as deliberately playing below one's ability. It is more common where organisers rely on self-ratings, and the fix is to gate events with verified ratings. — [DUPR FAQ (May 2024)](https://www.dupr.com/vi/post/upa-integration-and-dupr-algorithm-faqs---all-you-need-to-know)
- **No-show penalties can backfire.** Playtomic reviewers report being marked "no-show" and charged despite being present, and fined a full court fee after apologising in chat. — [Playtomic Trustpilot reviews](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/www.playtomic.io)

### Inferences
- Bink's per-court "king of the hill" could be a **persistent title** (the current holder is whoever last beat the previous holder at that court) or a **ranked leaderboard per court** (top rating among players with N confirmed matches there). The persistent-title model follows challenge ladder rules. To keep it fresh and fair it would need a challenge acceptance deadline and a default rule (as in the Luna Sol ladder). It would also need a minimum-activity rule so an inactive king does not hold the court forever.
- For doubles, a "king" is really a team, or both winners share the title. King-of-the-Court sessions rotate partners, so a persistent individual title is easier to defend when it is based on individual rating at that court rather than the last match won.
- **Verification:** Bink's "both players confirm" matches the "1 player per side" pattern. Adding an **auto-confirm after X hours unless disputed** (3 hours in the Dinkzter example) avoids results stuck in "pending" when the loser never confirms. That carries some risk of fake wins against inactive users. Ways to limit it: allow auto-confirm only with a reported score, count it at reduced weight, or let either side flag a dispute before the window ends.
- **Sandbagging and farming:** For win streaks and king titles, the main risks are padding wins against much weaker players and colluding pairs. Countermeasures drawn from the rating systems: weight results by opponent reliability (UTR), use expected-vs-actual scoring so beating weaker players gains little (DUPR, Elo), and cap repeated results against the same opponent. Published ladders do not describe these explicitly; this is an inference.
- **Disputes:** No app's dispute workflow was found. A simple Bink approach would be to mark disputed matches as "excluded from ratings and streaks" and track each player's dispute rate.

### Gaps
- No published dispute-resolution process from any ladder app (Global Pickleball Network, Dinkzter and others) was found.
- No source was found on how apps score "win streaks" (reset rules, whether doubles counts, minimum opponent level).
- The Spanish Fork 2-hour reporting window came from a truncated search snippet and was not verified in the PDF.

## 4. Signals that predict a good recreational match: skill gap, play times, distance, play style, reliability (no-shows)

### Takeaway
Across apps, the signals actually used are **skill band, distance or radius, availability, format (singles or doubles), gender or age, and favourite courts**. Community sources show **skill mismatch** as the main pain in open play. Playtomic's split between "friendly" and "competitive" matches is the clearest product example of matching on play-style intent. **Reliability or no-show** scoring exists only in a few small apps, and Playtomic's paid-booking enforcement draws complaints. No source gave numbers on which signals predict a good match.

### Cited Findings
- Filters used across partner apps: distance, skill, availability, gender, player type and singles or doubles (Hitch). Skill range, age range, location radius and favourite courts (PickleMatchApp). Configurable radius on a map (PairUp). — [Hitch](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6670320911); [PickleMatchApp](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/picklematchapp/id6749190103); [PairUp](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6755626973)
- Hitch reviewers asked for more **availability** and **experience-level** filters, a sign that these are the most-wanted signals. — [Hitch App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6670320911)
- Playtomic separates **friendly** (no level impact) from **competitive** (level-changing) open matches. Open matches carry a level range, and players outside it must request entry. — [Playtomic Friendly vs Competitive](https://helpmanager.playtomic.com/hc/en-gb/articles/20535188135185-Friendly-vs-Competitive-Open-Matches); [Request a spot](https://helpmanager.playtomic.com/hc/en-gb/articles/20535718052241-Request-a-spot-in-an-Open-Match)
- Playtomic says a wrong level "leads to poor experiences for the player, their partner, and their opponents". — [Playtomic leveling process](https://product.playtomic.com/en-us/new_leveling_process)
- Open play rewards risk-taking while tournaments punish it, so social and competitive players want different things from the same session. One or two players out of their depth can derail an advanced session. — [The Dink: 4.0 open play](https://www.thedinkpickleball.com/the-harsh-truth-about-4-0-open-play/); [Pickleball Union](https://pickleballunion.com/how-true-is-rated-open-play-in-pickleball/)
- PlayTime Scheduler lets players see "where your friends are playing", using the social graph as a matching signal alongside skill. — [PlayTime Scheduler](https://playtimescheduler.com/learn-more.php)
- **Reliability or no-shows:** a third-party aggregator says the "Tennis Buddy: Local Play" app tracks match completion and no-show history to give every profile a public reliability score. This comes from an aggregator, not the developer, and the app looks inactive. — [Tennis Buddy listing (aggregator)](https://marlvel.ai/api/llm/apps/sports/tennis-buddy-local-play)
- Playtomic users report being wrongly marked as no-shows and fined, which shows the risks of automated no-show enforcement. — [Playtomic Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/www.playtomic.io)
- TrueSkill defines a good match as a **high draw probability**, meaning close predicted outcome. — [Microsoft Research TrueSkill](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/trueskill-ranking-system/)

### Inferences
- A practical "for you" score for Bink's directory, built from the signals above, would combine:
  - skill closeness: a soft penalty growing with the rating gap, with tolerance about ±0.5 for social play and tighter for competitive, using rating uncertainty when available;
  - shared home courts or court-visit overlap, Bink's strongest local signal, since courts already fold in distance;
  - play-time overlap;
  - play-style match (fun/social/competitive, following Playtomic's friendly-versus-competitive split);
  - reliability (confirmed matches, low dispute rate);
  - social proximity (played together before, or common opponents, as in PlayTime's "friends").
  The weights would need tuning; no source gives them.
- Distance probably matters less within one city than in multi-city apps. Overlapping courts and play times are likely stronger signals for Victoria.
- For reliability, a softer signal is safer than Playtomic-style penalties. Examples: a public "confirmed matches" count, or a private reliability score used only for ranking.

### Gaps
- No published study or app data was found that measures how much each signal predicts satisfaction or repeat play.
- No source was found on what skill gap recreational players will tolerate (for example, ±0.5 DUPR). The ±0.5 above is an inference.
- No-show rates in pickleball or tennis partner apps were not found.

## 5. Cold start and network effects: seeding a city; court-centric versus player-centric discovery

### Takeaway
Little published evidence covers how local racket-sport apps bootstrap. The clearest patterns are **supply-side seeding through clubs, facilities and organisers**, such as DUPR's 15,000+ organisers submitting results, Playtomic auto-creating open matches at club slots, and Pickleheads' court directory. Partner-finding apps without that base are reported to feel empty. For Victoria, the seeding points are the city's drop-in courts (Topaz, Beacon Hill), VRPA drop-in sessions and existing Meetup groups.

### Cited Findings
- Playtomic auto-generates open matches at a club's available slots, so a club's empty inventory becomes joinable matches without anyone organising them. — [Playtomic: configure Open Matches](https://helpmanager.playtomic.com/hc/en-gb/articles/20535035123473-How-to-configure-Open-Matches-at-your-Club)
- Playtomic presents club leveling sessions as bringing "more revenue, higher occupancy, new player acquisition" to clubs, so the club is the place new players are acquired. — [Playtomic leveling process](https://product.playtomic.com/en-us/new_leveling_process)
- DUPR grew data through organisers ("over 15,000 event organizers submit match results") and imported historical results from Pickleballtournaments.com and Pickleball Brackets (Dec 2024). — [DUPR How It Works](https://dupr.com/how-it-works); [DUPR 2024 updates](https://www.dupr.com/post/2024-dupr-product-algorithm-updates)
- Pickleheads seeded with a court directory of more than 16,000 locations before it needed players at each one. — [Pickleheads App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/id6448714446)
- A partner-app reviewer said the local model "might be an isolating experience because it might take a long time until people nearside your area show interest unless they are already on the app". — [Tennis app review via search summary](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6760328750)
- **Victoria, BC context:**
  - Topaz Park has drop-in-only courts (courts 1–3) and reservable courts (4–11). The City says drop-in players must leave reservable courts when a booking starts. Beacon Hill Park also has drop-in courts. — [City of Victoria pickleball](https://www.victoria.ca/node/3176); [Pickleheads Topaz Park](https://www.pickleheads.com/courts/ca/british-columbia/victoria/topaz-park)
  - The **Victoria Regional Pickleball Association (VRPA)**, a Pickleball Canada club, lists drop-in at CARSA Gym (UVic, Tue–Sun), Cordova Bay Community Club (Tue/Fri pm), a Saanich session through Silver Threads (Mon/Wed/Fri 1–3pm), Victoria Pickleball Centre in Langford (6 dedicated courts, reservations) and Juan de Fuca Rec Centre (8 courts, through WestShore Rec). — [VRPA where to play](https://secure.pickleballcanada.org/club/victoria-regional-pickleball-association/1033/where-to-play/7652/)
  - The Victoria Pickleball and Social Club Meetup group runs free casual play at Beacon Hill Park courts on Mon/Wed/Fri at about 11am. — [Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/victoria-pickleball-and-social-club/events/tgbsrtyhckbfc)
  - James Bay Community School Centre runs unsupervised adult (16+) drop-in: pay and sign in, no pre-registration. — [Active.com listing (2024)](https://www.active.com/victoria-bc/classes/drop-in-pickleball-16-2024)
  - UVic Vikes Rec runs pickleball programming. — [Vikes Rec pickleball](https://ns2.vikesrec.ca/pickleball)
  - Esquimalt's FAQ names a "Greater Victoria Pickleball Association", which may be an older name or a conflicting source. — [Esquimalt pickleball FAQ](https://www.esquimalt.ca/node/8491)

### Inferences
- A **court-first** design helps with cold start. Courts exist and can be listed on day one, before any players join, which is the approach Pickleheads used. Each court page is useful as soon as it has 3–5 regulars, while a citywide player directory needs many more users before it is useful. Drop-in courts (Topaz 1–3, Beacon Hill) are where strangers already gather to play, so they are natural first "hills".
- Seeding approach suggested by the patterns above: partner with VRPA or Meetup organisers at a few drop-in courts. Get regulars to tag those courts as home and log a few confirmed matches so the king of the hill and court regular lists fill up. Then let the "Find players in Victoria" directory build on that activity. The directory is better treated as a secondary view until density exists.
- Court-centric discovery also fits how Victoria play is organised today: fixed drop-in times at specific courts (VRPA, Meetup, city courts). Playing-time overlap can be read partly from court plus schedule.

### Gaps
- No case study or data was found on how Playtomic, Pickleheads, PlayTime Scheduler or any partner app launched in a specific city, or what density threshold made the network useful.
- No source compared retention between court-centric and player-centric discovery.
- The number of active pickleball players in Victoria and VRPA membership size were not found. Pickleball Canada and BC Pickleball membership numbers were not researched for lack of time.
