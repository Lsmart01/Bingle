# Borrow Timeleft's warmth, paint it court blue

Bink should model its marketing site on the **system** the best dating and meet-people sites share, not on any single one of them. That system is one owned accent colour, warm tinted neutrals instead of pure black and white, an expressive headline face over a plain sans, candid local photography, and one repeated call to action. The most useful references are **Timeleft** for structure, copy and warmth, **Hinge** for restraint and its 90/10 colour discipline, **Bumble** for its profile-card hero, **Cerca and Lex** for friendly, non-romantic tone, and **Tinder** for witty closing copy. Feeld is the craft benchmark but its tone is too sensual to copy. For colour, the recommendation is a **blue-led court palette ("Outdoor Court": court blue #1D5FA8, pro green #2D7A4B, Line White #F8FAF7, ball yellow #F4E04D, warm ink #15233A)**, adjusted to answer the strongest objection to it: blue, especially navy, is the most crowded colour in pickleball, and a mid court green is open territory. Bink resolves that by using a mid, daylight court blue and never navy, by giving green a visible brand role rather than a token one, and by building distinctiveness from the whole court system (blue, green, white lines, yellow ball) on warm white, not from one hue. A green-led alternative is viable but has fixable accessibility weaknesses and a smaller brand payoff than it first appears. Every contrast figure below was computed against WCAG 2.2 thresholds; none of the palette choices has been tested with real players yet.

## Five sites earn a place on Bink's moodboard

No dating-app marketing site turned up among Awwwards, CSSDA or FWA winners. Design credibility in this category comes from **identity systems praised by design press**, not web-award effects such as WebGL or scroll-jacking ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/incinq-dating-application-case)). That is good news for a small startup: the things worth copying are cheap. The table ranks the references by how much Bink should take from each.

| Site | What Bink should borrow | Why it fits Bink |
|---|---|---|
| **Timeleft** | Text-led hero ("The weekly gatherings turning strangers into friends"), triple-negative promise ("No bios. No swiping. No planning."), 3-step "How it works", activity cards, quiz-card sign-up, warm cream ground | Closest positioning: meeting strangers in person, friendly, no dating language |
| **Hinge** | 90% black/white, 10% accent rule; semibold serif headlines over a sans; all-caps tracked CTA labels; "honest and authentic" photography | The category's taste benchmark; its palette "draws from the outdoors" |
| **Bumble** | Rotating hero stack of profile cards (name, age, candid photo); 16px card radius and pill buttons | Directly reusable as player cards: name, skill level, usual court |
| **Cerca / Lex** | "Silly, loose and very human" characters or a sticker system; spring colours instead of clichés | Praised recent work that is explicitly social and non-sexual |
| **Tinder** | Self-aware closing band ("You've read enough. Go find out."), marquee strip, safety grid | Strong voice; safety matters when meeting strangers to play |
| Feeld | Craft reference only: testimonial cards, "Join a community of 14M+" avatar pill, in-person event cards | Too sensual to copy tonally |

Timeleft is the closest model. Its hero is centred and text-led, its sub-copy defuses anxiety ("Don't be nervous"), and its steps are actions: choose your activity, book your spot, show up ([Timeleft](https://www.timeleft.com)). Bink can mirror this almost line for line, e.g. "No group chats. No no-shows. Just games at your level." Timeleft's palette also demonstrates the formula the best friendly sites converge on: **deep navy ink #181F32, orange #F97709 accent, and warm stone/cream #F2EFE9** ([Timeleft](https://www.timeleft.com)). Breeze and happn follow the same pattern with plum or near-black ink on peach or cream grounds ([Breeze](https://www.breeze.social); [happn](https://www.happn.com)).

Hinge supplies the discipline. Its guidelines set **Hinge Black #1A1A1A and Hinge White #FFFEFD, "intentionally not pure… for an organic feel"**, and require black and white to cover at least 90% of a space with accents capped at 10%. Headlines use Tiempos Headline Semibold at 110% leading, body uses Modern Era at 140%, and CTA labels are bold all caps tracked at 150 ([Hinge brand guidelines](https://hinge-preview.vercel.app/brand)). Its homepage spends its space on mission, research, testimonials and press rather than features ([hinge.co](https://hinge.co)).

Bumble provides the most reusable hero. A rotating stack of candid profile cards ("Danna, 28", "Josh, 34") shows the product working without an app screenshot ([Bumble](https://bumble.com/en/)). For Bink, "Maya, 3.5, plays Tuesday mornings at Oak Bay" does the same job. Cerca and Lex prove a friendly tone can still be stylish. It's Nice That praised Cerca's characters as "intentionally silly, loose and very human" and noted the team avoided heavy safety messaging to keep things light ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/saint-urbain-cerca-animation-graphic-design-project-020326)). &Walsh's Lex rebrand pivoted to platonic connection, with "fresh 'spring' colors" chosen over rainbow tropes and stickers for interests ([Abduzeedo](https://abduzeedo.com/node/87696); [&Walsh](https://andwalsh.com/work/all/lex/)). Lex-style stickers translate directly to skill tags such as "3.0", "dinker" or "early bird".

The counter-examples are just as instructive. The League's WordPress build, FontAwesome icons, teal #1DDFD2 on #272727 and "ARE YOU IN?" gatekeeping copy read dated ([The League](https://www.theleague.com)), and Boo's mix of Roboto, Roboto Serif, Bebas Neue and Kaushan Script is typeface soup ([Boo](https://www.boo.world)). Raya-style waitlists built on exclusivity work as marketing for elite brands ([Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/raya-built-a-25-million-person-waitlist-without-spending-a-dollar-on-marketing-and-that-is-the-business-lesson/)) but contradict "friendly and social". Bink's beta should use one low-friction email CTA, repeated down the page as Timeleft and Feeld do, with communal rather than gatekept scarcity: "Launching first in Victoria" and a running count of local players.

## Every admired site runs on one hue and warm neutrals

The colour lesson from the big three is consistent. **Each owns one hue and uses it sparingly against near-black and off-white.** Tinder's live CSS declares pink-red #FD267A as its theme colour with a gradient to #FF6036; Bumble's primary button is yellow #FFC629 with #141414 text on a #FCFCFC page; Hinge stays roughly 90% black and off-white ([tinder.com](https://tinder.com); [bumble.com](https://bumble.com/en/); [Hinge brand guidelines](https://hinge-preview.vercel.app/brand)). None uses pure #000 or #FFF for main text and backgrounds.

Two further signals matter for Bink's palette. First, Tinder's July 2026 Porto Rocha rebrand added **blue and green to cover "a wider spectrum of the emotions"**, including soft tints such as haze blue #9DB9E6 ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/porto-rocha-tinder-rebrand-graphic-design-spotlight-140726); [tinder.com](https://tinder.com)). Big brands are softening loud primaries with gentle secondaries, the move Bink needs to make with green. Second, colour alone does not create friendliness. Bumble's 2024 rebrand refreshed logo, type, colour and illustration, yet brand perception fell sharply, driven mostly by campaign messaging ([YouGov](https://business.yougov.com/content/51733-from-rebrand-to-controversy-bumbles-brand-health-struggles-in-2024-2025)). Voice, photography and copy do as much work as hue.

Typography follows the same convergence. Hinge pairs Tiempos Headline with Modern Era; Tinder's 2026 system adds a serif ("Society") over a sans ("Regola") and keeps Proxima Nova for body ([tinder.com](https://tinder.com); [Hinge brand guidelines](https://hinge-preview.vercel.app/brand)). The friendlier, design-led sites swap the serif for a characterful display face: Timeleft uses FeatureDeck Extrabold Italic over FixelText, and Breeze uses Apris Black over DM Sans ([Timeleft](https://www.timeleft.com); [Breeze](https://www.breeze.social)). For Bink, a free rounded or chunky display face over DM Sans or Inter suits "friendly and sporty" better than an editorial serif, which reads premium and romantic. Pastel "aura" gradients (Feeld), heart and spark motifs, and exclusivity language should be avoided because they all signal dating ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/feeld-rebrand-graphic-design-141123)).

## Court colours have no standard, so Bink sets its own values

There is no official pickleball court colour scheme, but the convention is clear: **a blue playing area with green surrounds and white lines**, and USA Pickleball recommends white lines because they "contrast with almost any color" ([The Pickleball Source](https://thepickleballsource.com/pickleball-court-colors/)). Coating supplier Acrytech names Competition Green, Pro Green, Pro Blue, Standard Blue and Light Blue as the most common colours ([PPA Tour blog](https://www.ppatour.com/ppa-blog/pickleball-court-colors-acrytech-has-you-covered/)), and SportMaster's featured court pairs a light green court with a light blue kitchen and white lines ([SportMaster](https://www.sportmaster.net/courtdesigner/pickle-ball/)). No manufacturer publishes hex values, and colours vary with site conditions and mixing ([California Sports Surfaces](https://www.californiasportssurfaces.com/custom-color-program/)).

That freedom matters. Real court acrylics are matte, mid-value and somewhat greyed, so a literal screen reproduction looks drab. Bink's values are therefore design choices inspired by courts, not measurements. A photo audit of Victoria courts would let the brand match "your" court, which no competitor has claimed.

Colour theory supports the founder's brief with one condition. Blue and green are **analogous hues**: they harmonise easily but turn muddy when they share lightness and saturation, so they need a clear value gap and should never carry text on each other. Research on brand colour links blue with competence and trustworthiness and warm hues with excitement ([Labrecque & Milne 2012](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s11747-010-0245-y); [Elliot 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00368/full)), though Elliot cautions that the field is "at a nascent stage" and context shapes meaning. A blue-green-white scheme has almost no temperature contrast and drifts towards "hospital, fintech, SaaS". Headspace's refresh is a cautionary example: it added orange to escape a "dreary sea of blues and greys" ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/italic-studio-headspace-graphic-design-project-250424)). Bink can solve this without leaving the court because **the ball itself is yellow**. A small yellow accent, kept to about 10% under the 60-30-10 rule where the accent is reserved for "buttons, links, CTAs" ([UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/the-60-30-10-rule-a-simple-way-to-creating-catchy-user-interfaces-e9e2cf957213)), adds warmth that is native to the sport.

## Blue is crowded and green is open, but blue should still lead

This is where the two lines of research conflict, and the conflict is real.

**The case for green.** A scrape of competitor sites found that **five of nine scrapeable pickleball and racket brands lead with navy or blue**: DUPR #05155E, PPA Tour #0C2B44, Major League Pickleball #0A2137, Franklin #10162C and Playtomic's electric blue #335FFF over navy ([DUPR](https://dupr.com); [PPA Tour](https://ppatour.com); [MLP](https://majorleaguepickleball.co); [Franklin](https://franklinpickleball.com); [Playtomic](https://playtomic.com)). Neon ball yellow-green is the default accent (Playtomic #C8FC2C, PlayYourCourt #E8FF3D) ([PlayYourCourt](https://playyourcourt.com)). The pickleball identities design press has praised reject this look. Picklewood in Seattle built a retro country-club identity on court-derived **green and clay** ([Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/inspiration/picklewood/)). On distinctiveness alone, a mid court green is the more ownable lead colour.

**The case for blue.** The palette analysis recommends blue for four reasons. Blue is the playing surface, so it is the most recognisable "court" cue. It carries trust, which matters when an app asks people to meet strangers. White text passes comfortably on the chosen blue (6.45:1). And it gives the strongest colour-vision separation. It flags three problems with the green-led variant: its green-700 #2F7D52 and blue-600 #2C72B8 share the same lightness (L* 47), so they become confusable in greyscale and for people with tritanopia (ΔE about 12); a green brand colour collides with green success states; and bright green invites Duolingo comparisons (Duolingo's Feather Green is #58CC02 ([Canny Creative](https://www.canny-creative.com/brand-breakdown/brand/duolingo/))). Contrast was checked with the WCAG relative-luminance formula against [W3C SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html) thresholds; the colour-vision figures come from standard simulation matrices and still need checking in a tool such as Stark.

**Weighing them.** Neither argument is decisive alone, and both are weaker than they look. On the green side, green is not empty: CourtReserve already uses lime #94DE64 and forest greens ([CourtReserve](https://courtreserve.com)). And V4's accessibility flaws are tuning problems, not structural ones, since any blue and green can be separated in lightness. On the blue side, the crowding is concentrated in **dark navy** (four of the five blue leaders sit at very low lightness) and in "performance" navy-plus-neon looks. A mid, daylight court blue on warm white with green and a soft lemon yellow does not read like a tour or rating app. The overlap risk is real at the bright end, though: DUPR also uses bright blues such as #2B63C2 and #3A79E0, close to Bink's #1D5FA8 ([DUPR](https://dupr.com)). Blue alone will not make Bink distinctive.

| | V1 "Outdoor Court" (blue-led) | V4 "Kitchen Line" (green-led) |
|---|---|---|
| Lead hue | Court Blue #1D5FA8 | Pro Green #2F7D52 |
| Distinct from pickleball rivals | Weak on hue, strong if kept off navy and paired with green and yellow | Strong on hue; CourtReserve partly overlaps |
| Recognisable as "a court" | Strongest (the playing surface is blue) | Good (green courts are common) |
| White on primary CTA | 6.45:1 | 5.03:1 |
| Blue-vs-green separation | L* 40 vs 46; strong for protan and deutan (ΔE ≥ 70), weak for tritan (ΔE 13.7) | L* 47 vs 47; confusable in greyscale and for tritanopes (ΔE 12.2) |
| Semantic conflict | None | Brand green vs success green |
| Feel | Cheerful, trustworthy, sporty | Outdoorsy, Pacific Northwest, calmer |

**Recommendation: lead with blue, but adjusted.** The decisive factor is that Bink's brand job is to signal "pickleball, friendly, safe to show up" in one glance to people who mostly do not know any of these competitors. A blue court with green surrounds, white lines and a yellow ball says that most directly. Distinctiveness should come from the system and the warmth, not from avoiding blue. Three adjustments answer the crowding concern. First, **never use navy as a brand surface**: keep the hero, logo and CTA in the mid court blue, and keep dark mode or footers from becoming the navy band every competitor uses. Second, **promote green from token to co-star**: use Pro Green for full-width section bands, illustration and the logo's secondary element, so green sits nearer 25–30% of the coloured area rather than the 10–15% V1 originally gave it. Third, keep the accent a **soft lemon ball yellow (#F4E04D), not neon lime**, which avoids both the Playtomic and PlayYourCourt neon and Bumble's honey #FFC629.

Choose green-led instead if the founder rates instant distinctiveness among pickleball apps above instant court recognition, or if the logo is green. In that case, fix V4 before shipping it: darken or lighten the blue to open at least a 10 L* gap from the green, give success states a separate shade with icons, and stay away from Duolingo's bright yellow-green. A five-second preference test with 15–20 Victoria players, comparing the adjusted V1 with a corrected V4, would settle the question cheaply. No user testing exists yet for either.

## The palette Bink should ship

The tokens below are V1 "Outdoor Court" with V2's softer tinted section backgrounds borrowed for warmth, as the palette notes suggest. | Token | Hex | Role | Key contrast |
|---|---|---|---|

| blue-50 / blue-100 | #EAF2FB / #DCEAF8 | Light "kitchen" section backgrounds | Ink on blue-50 13.94 |
| Pro Green (green-700) | #2D7A4B | Section bands, badges, logo secondary | White on it 5.25; on page 5.01 |
| green-500 | #4FA36B | Illustration; large type with ink | Ink on it 5.09 |
| green-50 / green-100 | #E8F4EC / #DDF0E3 | Light "surround" section backgrounds | Ink on green-50 13.94 |
| Ball Yellow | #F4E04D | Accent CTA (ink text), highlights, ball motif | Ink on it 11.71; shape on page 1.28 |
| Line White (page) | #F8FAF7 | Page background, about 60% of the page | — |
| Surface | #FFFFFF | Cards | — |
| Ink | #15233A | Body and headings | 15.01 on page |
| Ink muted | #4B5A6B | Secondary text | 6.73 on page |
| Border input | #7C8A82 | Form controls | 3.61 on white |
| Error | #C0392B | Errors, always with an icon | 5.18 on page |

A few rules keep the palette accessible and friendly. **Yellow always carries dark ink text, never white**, and because a yellow button on Line White is only 1.28:1 as a shape, it needs an ink border or must sit on a blue or green band. **Court Blue and Pro Green never carry text on each other** (1.23:1); separate them with Line White or put white text on each. Meaning must never rest on "blue versus green" alone, because that pair is weak for tritanopes. Under protanopia the success-green versus error-red pair collapses (ΔE 12.0), so status messages need icons and words ("✓ Matched", "⚠ Fix this"). These follow standard guidance that colours carrying meaning differ in lightness, not just hue ([Data Europa](https://data.europa.eu/apps/data-visualisation-guide/accessible-colour-palettes)). The warm Line White rather than pure #FFFFFF matches both Hinge's "intentionally not pure" approach and the 2026 shift to softer whites, with Pantone's 2026 colour itself a white, Cloud Dancer ([Galerie](https://galeriemagazine.com/pantone-announces-cloud-dancer-color-of-the-year-2026/)). Dark mode works but tests the "no navy" rule: the computed V1 dark set (background #0F1A2A, text #EEF2F0 at 15.48:1, blue-300 #7FB2EE at 7.91:1, green-300 #7CCB98 at 9.03:1) is accessible, but it should stay a user preference, not the marketing site's default look.

For context only: the earlier non-court directions (warm cream grounds with terracotta, coral or orange accents) are what the competitor research flags as the most open territory. They are superseded by the founder's court-colour brief. Their lesson survives in the warm Line White, warm ink and ball-yellow accent, which carry the "friendly" job those palettes were chosen for.

## Conclusion

The competitor scan and the palette analysis disagree less than they seem. Both conclude that Bink must not look like a navy-and-neon tour brand. The scan reads that as "avoid blue"; the closer reading is "avoid dark blue and neon, and don't rely on any single hue". The best dating sites show that ownership comes from discipline (one accent, warm neutrals, consistent voice) more than from claiming an empty hue. Hinge owns a near-monochrome palette and Timeleft shares navy with half of pickleball, yet both are unmistakable. Bink's distinctive asset is the full court-and-ball composition on warm white, delivered with Timeleft's copy and Bumble's player cards. No rival has packaged that as friendly rather than competitive.

The open questions are empirical and cheap to close. Exact court values from Victoria courts, a five-second test of blue-led against green-led with local players, and a simulator pass on the final tokens would replace the remaining judgement calls with evidence before the beta launches. The competitor sample also missed Pickleheads, PickleballBrackets and the local Victoria clubs, whose sites could not be scraped, so a quick visual check of those should come before the palette is locked.
