# Topaz and Fowler redraw Victoria's court map

As of November 2026, Greater Victoria has **roughly 70 public outdoor pickleball courts at about 25 sites, around 50 of them dedicated**. On top of that come about 20 indoor venues: municipal gyms, rec centres, the UVic fieldhouse and one commercial facility. Supply is concentrated in four hubs. **Topaz Park** in the City of Victoria has 11 dedicated, lit courts and opened in May 2026. **Saanich's Fowler Park** has 6 dedicated courts and had a soft launch in September 2026. **Oak Bay's Carnarvon Park** has 5 dedicated courts. **West Shore Parks & Recreation (WSPR) in Colwood** has 6 dedicated courts. The rest is a long tail of tennis courts painted with pickleball lines. Noise is what decides where courts can go. North Saanich closed its only courts at **Wain Road Park in May 2024**. Saanich closed **Tolmie Park on 1 October 2026**. Victoria removed the lines at Todd Park in 2022. Every new hub since then has been placed 50 m or more from homes. For Bink, the venues are not the main problem. Booking is: at least eight separate reservation systems, with fees from free to $29/hour, sit on top of a court network that changed a lot in 2025–26. The tables below are grouped by municipality and give addresses wherever a source provided one. Addresses taken only from third-party directories are marked †, and Section 5 lists the data points that sources disagree on.

## The core municipalities: venue by venue

### City of Victoria: Topaz anchors a bookable network

Victoria launched online court booking on **8 April 2026** ([City of Victoria](https://www.victoria.ca/node/3236)). Courts can be reserved up to 3 weeks ahead, in blocks of up to 3 hours, at **$10/hour for adults and $5/hour for youth**. Hours are 7 a.m. to dusk. Drop-in players must leave when a booking starts, and the courts at Beacon Hill are kept for drop-in only ([City of Victoria](https://www.victoria.ca/parks-recreation/recreation/outdoor-recreation-facilities)). Topaz opened under phase two of the park's improvement plan, with permanent nets, fencing, a shade structure and **lighting for evening play** ([City of Victoria](https://www.victoria.ca/node/3282)). The City runs no indoor pickleball of its own. **Crystal Pool closed permanently on 25 September 2026**. Its interim replacement, the Victoria Recreation Centre at 851 Broughton St, opens in early 2027 with a gymnasium, but no source mentions pickleball there ([City of Victoria](https://www.victoria.ca/node/3559)).

### Esquimalt: summer courts on curling ice, almost nothing outdoors

In the off-season, 4 pickleball courts are laid out on the **Archie Browning Sports Centre curling-rink floor**. In 2026 they ran from 23–24 May to 5 September, and they are due back in spring 2027. ### Saanich: one new hub, one closure and conversions on hold

Saanich has the most venues in the region. Its outdoor pickleball hours are **9 a.m.–8 p.m.**, the shared courts are first-come, first-served, and play is free except for the Fowler booking pilot ([Saanich](https://www.saanich.ca/EN/main/parks-recreation-community/parks/parks-trails-amenities/amenities/tennis-courts.html)). **Fowler Park** opened 15 September 2026 with 6 dedicated courts and 1 tennis court. During the pilot (15 Sept–31 Oct 2026), 4 of its courts could be reserved through Saanich RecOnline at **$10.50/hour**, up to 7 days ahead, while 2 stayed walk-up. Reservations are set to return in spring 2027 ([Saanich release](https://www.saanich.ca/EN/main/news-events/news-archives/2026-news/saanich-to-pilot-reservation-system-for-new-fowler-park-pickleball-courts.html)). The $2.4M site sits below road level behind berms, was designed with an acoustic engineer, and is set for its full opening in **late April 2027** ([Victoria News](https://vicnews.com/2026/09/08/here-to-stay-saanich-answers-pickleball-demand-with-fowler-park-soft-launch/)). Indoors, Saanich Commonwealth Place runs reserved drop-in. G.R. Pearkes and Cedar Hill rent courts to casual groups. The Cedar Hill rate is **$29.14/hour**, the highest public fee in the region ([Saanich Racquet Sports](https://www.saanich.ca/EN/main/parks-recreation-community/recreation/schedules/racquet-sports.html)).

### Oak Bay and View Royal: one flagship court site, and none at all

Oak Bay has a single outdoor venue. **Carnarvon Park, 2801 Henderson Rd**, has 5 dedicated courts in the former lacrosse box, open 9 a.m.–9 p.m. Bookings cost **$11/hour in daylight and $18.50/hour for lighted play**, and anyone may walk on when a court is not booked ([Oak Bay](https://www.oakbay.ca/parks-recreation/facilities-fields/bookings/book-a-court/)). ## The peninsula and the West Shore: roofed courts, new hubs and the one private centre

### Saanich Peninsula: Centennial Park took over after Wain Road closed

**Centennial Park in Saanichton (1859 Hovey Rd)** is now the peninsula's hub. Its **Multi-Sport Box** opened 2 September 2025 with a roof and LED lighting. That makes its 4 courts the only covered outdoor pickleball courts in the region and the region's best wet-weather option. The CRD runs it through Panorama Recreation ([Central Saanich](https://www.centralsaanich.ca/node/3617)). Across the road, Council approved relining **Court #1** into 4 dedicated courts in June 2026, raising the District's total to 8 ([Central Saanich](https://www.centralsaanich.ca/node/3989)). The current bookings page lists courts 1A–1D, which suggests the work is finished, though no completion notice was found. Adults pay $6.75/hour to book 1A and 1B, and the courts are "for pickleball only" ([Central Saanich bookings](https://www.centralsaanich.ca/node/220)). Panorama releases indoor and Open Court sessions each Friday for the following week and lends paddles and eye protection free of charge ([Panorama](https://www.panoramarecreation.ca/drop-activities/sports)).

## Noise keeps closing courts near homes, and new hubs go elsewhere

All three of the region's outright closures came from neighbour noise complaints. Each was followed by a purpose-built hub at a distance from homes. **Victoria** removed the lines from the Todd Park tennis courts on 31 March 2022; the courts sit about 17 m from the nearest home ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/8702514/victoria-pickleball-group-ousted-noise-complaints)). The City then said its dedicated Topaz courts would go where noise would not affect residents ([CTV](https://vancouverisland.ctvnews.ca/pickleball-s-growth-raises-a-racket-in-victoria-amid-bans-over-noise-complaints-1.5883854)). **North Saanich** chose permanent closure of Wain Road in April 2024 rather than spend $50K–70K on sound fencing ([North Saanich](https://northsaanich.ca/post/wain-road-park-pickleball-courts); [CHEK](https://www.cheknews.ca/this-was-not-a-snap-decision-north-saanich-votes-to-close-wain-park-pickleball-courts-1202368/)). Supporters dropped their bid for a judicial review in February 2025 ([Capital Daily](https://www.capitaldaily.ca/news/judicial-review-request-rescinded-north-saanich-pickleball-saga-plays-on)). That same year, a 400-signature petition blocked courts at Cy Hampson Park ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/10683796/pickleball-controversy-north-saanich/amp)). **Saanich's Tolmie Park** was the District's first dedicated site, opened in 2016. Complaints started in 2017 and continued through sound panels in 2018 and time limits in 2022. Council had committed to close Tolmie once Fowler opened, and it closed on 1 October 2026 ([Saanich open house](https://www.saanich.ca/assets/Parks~Recreation~and~Community~Services/Documents/Pickleball-Tennis-OpenHouse-FINAL.pdf)). Saanich now rules out courts within 50 m of homes without major sound abatement and requires mitigation from 50 to 110 m.

Oak Bay is the exception. It has kept its courts open by spending money. In 2021 it moved them into the lacrosse box for up to $70K, and in 2022 it added about $25K of acoustic fencing ([Victoria News](https://vicnews.com/2022/06/24/oak-bay-battles-pickleball-noise-with-rubber-fencing/)). Even so, the 2024 regional strategy rates Carnarvon's 33 m setback as below standard. Its permanent fix is a **park redevelopment with 5 pickleball and 3 tennis courts behind acoustic panels**. Design packages were due in summer 2026, the work is phased over about six years, and the courts are expected to be **out of service for an extended period** during construction ([Oak Bay News](https://oakbaynews.com/2025/05/02/oak-bay-council-moves-ahead-with-plans-for-major-upgrade-to-carnarvon-park/)). No construction date was found.

Several other projects are still pending. Saanich plans to convert McMinn to 4 dedicated courts with sound mitigation and a shelter, "tentatively 2026." Conversions from shared to dedicated at Copley West, Hampton and Rosedale are all **on hold** ([Saanich plan](https://www.saanich.ca/EN/main/parks-recreation-community/parks/projects-in-saanich-parks/pickleball-and-tennis-in-outdoor-parks.html)). Saanich's long-run target is 16 dedicated and 2 shared courts. Sooke has a long-standing proposal for "at least three" courts beside the sports box at 2430 Phillips Rd, but no evidence shows it was built ([Sooke News Mirror](https://www.sookenewsmirror.com/news/plans-afoot-for-more-pickleball-courts-in-sooke/)). A $674K Blue Heron Park replacement for Wain Road in North Saanich was discussed but never confirmed ([Oak Bay News](https://www.oakbaynews.com/sports/north-saanichs-blue-heron-park-could-host-pickleball-courts-529314)). Victoria's interim Victoria Recreation Centre opens in early 2027, and the permanent Crystal Pool replacement, which includes a gym, is due by late 2030 ([City of Victoria](https://www.victoria.ca/city-government/projects/crystal-pool-replacement-project)). Either could become a City-run indoor site, but neither has a pickleball commitment.

### Clubs: one regional umbrella and a few local groups

The **VRPA** is the regional club. It has more than 1,000 members, charges $10 plus provincial and national fees, and was consulted on the Topaz design. It owns no courts ([VRPA benefits](https://secure.pickleballcanada.org/club/victoria-regional-pickleball-association/1033/member-benefits/3432/); [Saanich News](https://saanichnews.com/2026/05/08/vancouver-islands-largest-pickleball-hub-opens-at-victorias-topaz-park/)). On the peninsula there are two organisations: the **Saanich Peninsula Pickleball Association** ([Pickleball Canada](https://secure.pickleballcanada.org/club/saanich-peninsula-pickleball-association/1500)) and the membership-based **Peninsula Community Pickleball Club**, which reserves indoor and outdoor venues year-round ([OpenSports](https://opensports.net/peninsula-community-pickleball-club)). The **Sooke Pickleball Club** plays at Throup Road. Smaller groups include the Cordova Bay Community Club group, the Silver Threads seniors and Meetup groups such as "Victoria Pickleball and Social Club" ([Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/victoria-pickleball-and-social-club/events/bwldttyjcgbfc/)).

## Conclusion

The 2026 build-out turned Greater Victoria from a region of contested tennis-court lines into one with a small number of dedicated, bookable hubs: Topaz, Fowler, Carnarvon, WSPR, Royal Bay and Centennial. Those hubs are where games will concentrate. They are also where Bink's matchmaking matters most, because each now combines a paid reservation window with walk-up courts that players have to share. The booking landscape is fragmented. The City of Victoria, Saanich RecOnline, Oak Bay, WSPR's QR system, Central Saanich's Univerus portal, Panorama's Friday releases, Esquimalt and the Victoria Pickleball Centre's PlayByPoint each run their own window, from 2 days to 3 weeks ahead. No single tool shows a player where a game is happening tonight, and that gap is the product opportunity.

The inventory will keep changing in predictable ways. Expect more lost shared courts (Majestic is gone; Copley, Hampton and Rosedale are queued for conversion). Expect a long Carnarvon outage when Oak Bay starts construction, and seasonal switches in Esquimalt and Saanich's Fowler pilot. Winter is the binding constraint. Outside the lit Topaz courts and the roofed Sport Box in Saanichton, wet-season play depends on scarce gym time and one private centre in Langford. That makes indoor session data the most valuable and the most perishable part of a Bink venue database. Store each venue with its operator as the source of truth, a last-verified date and an explicit status field (open, seasonal, restricted, closed, planned). In this market, a court can be lined, relined or locked within a single season.
